All 2-cell embryos in the anchor's frame
The anchor sets both things the blastomere axis cannot: which blastomere is drawn on the right (the one holding more of the anchor gene per µm³, or nearer the polar body), and which way is up (the anchor's own direction). Each sperm is then snapped to the average outline of the blastomere it sits in.
Anchors that produce the same picture
Two anchors agree when they put the sperm in the same place. Each square is the mean of cos(Δθ) over the embryos both anchors can orient — +1 (dark) means the two cross-sections are interchangeable, −1 means one is the other turned inside out. Pale squares are pairs with too little overlap to compare; with disjoint probesets that is most of them, which is itself the point.
