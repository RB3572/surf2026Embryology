What all transcripts do along the axis
Figure 7.1. Before asking whether any gene leans one way, this asks what every transcript does. x runs from the junction (0) through the equal-volume split (0.5) to the cell edge (1), normalised per blastomere by that cell's own reach so embryo size divides out. A flat line at 0.05 would mean no axial structure at all. The fall-off at both ends is cell shape, not localisation — a bin at the junction and a bin at the far edge each sample less cytoplasm than one in the middle. That shape is exactly what the volcano and the maps then divide out.
Where the leaning sets actually sit
Figure 7.3. The top 15 genes each way, every transcript pooled over all early 2-cell blastomeres into one frame — horizontal is the junction→edge axis (dashed line = the equal-volume split), vertical is distance out from that axis — then divided by the same map built from every gene, so the two-lobed shape both sets share cancels. Illustrative, not a test: these sets were selected by their position on this very axis, so the gradients necessarily run the way they were selected to. It is here to show what a ±0.2 log₂ effect looks like in space, which is: barely anything.
One value per embryo
The per-embryo log₂(contact ÷ edge) the t-test runs on, after that embryo's own bulk split is subtracted. The halves are equal in volume, so this is a density ratio without needing a volume term. Scatter across embryos is the quantity the P-value is actually about.
