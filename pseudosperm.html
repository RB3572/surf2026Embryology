Pick a zygote
A green tab has a labelled sperm and gets its real sperm plane. The rest get a pseudosperm plane — the angle that best reproduces the split of the genes you select in the ranking on the right.
Cross-section at the plane
The cytoplasm outline in the plane's own frame, looking down the polar axis. The cut runs vertically; the selected genes' transcripts are coloured by which side they fall on. Pronuclei and the polar body are separate segments and never appear here, in any count, or in any volume.
Asymmetry across every angle
Every plane containing the polar axis, swept in 1° steps. The peak is the pseudosperm plane. Read the shape, not just the peak — a broad, flat curve means the angle is barely determined, and a bare number would hide that.
Per-side split at this plane
Each selected gene's transcripts either side of the cut, as raw counts and as concentration (count ÷ that side's cytoplasm volume). p is a two-sided exact binomial test against that cell's own volume split, so a gene counts as asymmetric only if it beats what the geometry alone would give.
Does the fit find the real sperm plane?
The only honest test of this whole idea. For the zygotes that do have a sperm, the fit is run anyway — ignoring the sperm — and the angle it lands on is compared with the true sperm plane. Chance is a flat line: two planes drawn at random differ by 45° on average, so 45° is the null, not 0°.
