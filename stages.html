Between-half fold change, three stages
Figure 9.1. One row per gene, coloured by the fold change itself. 1.0 means the two halves carry equal density, so the matrix is pale by construction — asymmetry at these stages is a small effect and the groups describe movement inside a narrow band. The grouping is imported: it is k-means on the stage-to-stage changes, so it cannot be read off the three fold values.
Count-matched percentile, early vs late
Figure 9.2. Percentile rather than raw asymmetry, because the asymmetry-to-noise ratio rises with transcript count — an unranked axis would mostly plot abundance. Each gene is ranked against genes within 3× its own abundance at the same stage, which is what makes the two axes comparable at all.
The three fates of a polarized transcript
Figure 8.6. Every gene measurable at both 2-cell stages is drawn in pale grey, so each fate is read against the same background rather than a hand-picked comparison group. Retained and lost are curated calls that survive both count adjustments; gained is derived by the mirror-image rule, so the three are commensurable. Late 2-cell carries only 6–15 embryos per gene — the noisiest quantity here — which is why no significance is claimed for any single line.

Fold is 2mean |log₂ ratio| over the embryos at that stage. null is the same arithmetic on pure counting noise — same totals, same volume ratios, redrawn sides — so a fold near its null is not asymmetry, it is arithmetic. n counts every embryo whose panel carries the gene; meas counts only those that actually detected it above the floor.
